Ana Chkhaidze

About me

I am a cognitive scientist and lecturer at the Free University of Tbilisi, currently based in Berlin. I recently completed my PhD in Cognitive Science at UC San Diego, where I worked with Lera Boroditsky, Anastasia Kiyonaga, and Seana Coulson. I am originally from Georgia—the country.

Research

I study individual differences in the formats of mental representations. People differ greatly in how they experience their thoughts: some think in vivid mental images, others rely more strongly on words, and some experience little or no visual imagery. Yet many theories of cognition assume that everyone has access to the same kinds of internal representations. Studying this diversity offers a way to understand how different underlying representational strategies may support cognition.

My work spans working memory, language, and perception. Across these domains, I combine behavioral experiments, EEG/ERP, and computational methods to examine how differences in representational formats are reflected in cognitive behavior and brain activity.
